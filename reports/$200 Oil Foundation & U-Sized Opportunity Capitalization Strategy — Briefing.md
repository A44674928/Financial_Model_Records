# $200 Oil Foundation & U-Sized Opportunity Capitalization Strategy — Briefing

## Executive framing

This portfolio is built around the **$200 OIL LONG**: the foundational BRENT/OIL position is the keystone, intended to remain locked while the market approaches the portfolio's $200 oil-price release condition. Around that immobile foundation, **U-sized trades** are the smaller, separate activity sleeve. Their strategic purpose is to pursue ROI-based, realized income during the period the oil foundation remains locked.

Together, these are the two parts of the **Fibonacci Volatility Cypher Shark Opportunity Capitalization Strategy**:

1. **Oil foundation — hold and protect.** Keep the foundational BRENT and OIL/USOIL long positions intact under the lock rule; do not rotate, trim, or treat them as the smaller opportunity-trading bankroll before the release condition is met.
2. **Opportunity capitalization — work around the foundation.** Run separately identified, U-sized strategies and models to seek ROI-based income. Keep their size, results, realized PnL, and risk distinct from the locked oil principal.

The strategy name describes the intended framework; it does **not** by itself define verified Fibonacci levels, volatility thresholds, Cypher/Shark pattern rules, order triggers, or guaranteed returns. Those operating rules must be specified before results can be attributed to a fully mechanical strategy.

## Opportunity-capitalization purpose and bookkeeping

The U-sized sleeve exists to seek ROI-based income **over the waiting period**—not to replace or liquidate the oil foundation. The intended accounting path is:

> Separately funded U-sized trade → close/realize the result → report realized PnL and ROI on the correct denominator → allocate any realized proceeds only under an explicit cash/reinvestment instruction.

- **Realized PnL only** is eligible to be called income or considered for redeployment. Unrealized gains are not cash income and do not automatically enlarge another sleeve.
- Keep **PnL / gross additions** separate from **PnL / funded cash or maximum gross-open notional**. Gross additions may reflect capital reused after closing positions; they are not automatically new outside cash.
- Keep each book's strategy/model identity, ticker, direction, entry/exit event, user-reported versus modeled status, supplied price/date, and TradingView mark provenance. Do not infer fills or undisclosed trades from a screenshot.
- Use the all-cash/debit convention: fully funded positions, no leverage, borrowing, or financing assumptions. If the U-sized sleeve or any pool allocation changes size, state the explicit scale factor and the new funded base.
- Do not net positions or silently transfer cash across foundation and opportunity sleeves. Any transfer of realized proceeds requires its own dated, auditable rule.
- Keep BRENT and OIL/USOIL as separate instruments and sleeves. Do not convert them to shares or barrels without the relevant contract multiplier; do not double-count DXY or any other sidecar already embedded in a composite.

The objective is ROI-based income, but **neither income nor profitability is assured**. A series of small trades can lose money, incur costs, or remain unrealized. Strategy performance needs a defined benchmark window, explicit denominators, drawdown reporting, and a separate record of realized versus unrealized results.

## Mechanics still to formalize

Before the full strategy can be reproduced mechanically or evaluated as one combined system, document the following without changing the locked foundation by implication:

1. The dollar amount and instrument exposure represented by one U in each opportunity sleeve; distinguish native U sizing from any pool-level $10K/$1B unit denominations.
2. The eligible strategy/model list, selection method, and whether any selection is random, signal-based, or discretionary.
3. The precise Fibonacci levels, volatility measure/window, Cypher/Shark pattern definitions, confirmation rules, entry/exit criteria, and invalidation conditions—if those are intended to be operative rules.
4. Sleeve-level and aggregate funded-capital limits, maximum gross open exposure, drawdown controls, and whether realized PnL may be reused.
5. The reporting cutoff and mark source for each instrument, plus a reconciliation process for user-reported paper actions versus modeled events and any separately verified executions.
6. The release procedure once both oil marks reach $200: which EOD marks qualify, how a crossing is timestamped, whether the foundation is sold or simply becomes eligible for review, and who authorizes any change. Until specified, the current rule is only “remain locked until both marks reach $200.”
