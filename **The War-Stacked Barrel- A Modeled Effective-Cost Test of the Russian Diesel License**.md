**The War-Stacked Barrel: A Modeled Effective-Cost Test of the Russian Diesel License**

*Independent analysis, October 20, 2026. Every figure here is a modeled quantity, not a price anyone paid. Inputs marked "unverified" came from the user or a satirical source and need independent checking.*

**1. Question.** Suppose a government spends heavily to support one side of a war and then eases a sanctions tool that lets the other side sell fuel. What is the effective cost of a barrel in use today if the earlier aid is credited with no value until a peace deal is verified?

**2. Facts the analysis rests on.**
- OFAC's General License 135 authorizes transactions involving Russian-origin diesel through April 7, 2027. Reporting says buyers, prices, payment and delivery dates were not disclosed.
- Bipartisan senators said the license would offer "limited to no relief" to Americans, and Axios reported that analysts doubt the promised relief.
- US military aid was $65.9 billion per the State Department (January 2025). Kiel puts all US aid near $127 billion (March 2026).
- Moving equipment used fuel. TRANSCOM reported 141,945 tonnes moved by 939 flights, 57 ships, 141 rail shipments and 5,500+ trucks through December 2022. My estimate is about 6 million barrels burned (range 2–15 million), roughly 1% of aid cost. This is an estimate scaled from 2022 data, not a measured total.

**3. Model.** Let V be the value credited to a donated barrel and L = P_donated − V the loss. Then **effective cost = P_used + L**. V is built from a baseline, a ceasefire credit with pre-defined verification rules, and negative terms such as new Russian revenue. It stays at zero until a verified outcome moves it. EIA's WTI averages price the donated barrels at $94.90 (2022), $77.58 (2023), $76.63 (2024) and $65.39 (2025).

**5. Summation.** Stacking all four years onto one barrel gives $424.50 on a crude basis and about $663 on a fully refined basis, but that charges multi-year losses to a single barrel, and, assumes that the war ends this year. Closing 2026 would increase multi-year losses once again.

**6. Offsetting evidence.**
- At the estimated price effect of about 2–5 cents per gallon (unverified), US savings over the license period are at most about $0.7–1.4B.

**7. Limits and process critique.**
- Barrel counts and equal yearly weights are placeholders. WTI is crude, while the fuel burned was refined and likely priced higher.
- Contested claims, including what the aid achieved and whether the license brings peace, are left to the reader.

**8. Audit conclusion.** On the stated assumptions, the modeled effective cost is about $193 per barrel in use, falling toward $110 as verified value accrues. A reviewer should replace the placeholders with agency fuel-purchase data and test the two unmeasured terms, V and f.
